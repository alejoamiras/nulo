Round 3Decided: dotted terms and the glossary. From your notes, the Fee Juice definitions are shorter, "sponsor" gets no tooltip, and the signatures definition is rewritten, which led me to rename the term. One word to confirm, and a rule for CLAUDE.md so the glossary stays current.

The four definitions

  • Public Fee Juice: "Paying a fee with it shows your address."
  • Private Fee Juice: "Paying a fee with it keeps your address hidden."
  • Rename for this app: "A private name for this account visible only to this app." Today's Alias tooltip.
  • Authorizations: "Lets a contract do one specific thing for you, once." It replaces "Permission for one of the app's contracts to make one specific call with your account."

Why a new word

  • It's the word Nulo's approval window already prints ("Authorizations the wallet will sign"), so the permission and the approval use the same noun.
  • With "signature" the definition has to explain two things: what a signature is, then what this one allows. "Authorization" names the thing itself.
  • Against it: other wallets say "signature", and people know the word. If you keep it, the new sentence still works: "Your OK for a contract to do one specific thing for you, once."

## Tooltips and the glossary

- Two kinds of tooltip only: the label of an icon-only button, or the definition of a dotted
  term. A warning or a choice is never a tooltip; it is visible text.
- Every definition lives in one module (proposed: `apps/extension/src/utils/glossary.ts`),
  which Settings → Glossary renders. A dotted term takes a glossary key, never its own text,
  so the tooltip and the glossary cannot disagree; a test fails on a key with no entry.
- A new dotted term or a changed definition is a UI change: owner sign-off, and the glossary
  entry lands in the same PR.
- At most two dotted terms per screen; one sentence, 100 characters at most.

My pick: Authorizations

The word decides how hard the definition has to work. "Authorization" says what it is, and it matches the approval window, where you'd meet one again. The key-not-text rule is the part that keeps the glossary current by construction; the CLAUDE.md lines only say why.

Confidence: moderate on the word; high on one module for every definition.
