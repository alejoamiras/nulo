Any-token bridge

Today the tools app can bridge exactly one token (USDC) because every piece — the Ethereum vault, the Aztec contract, the config file, the form — was built for one. This plan rebuilds the bridge so any ERC-20 on Ethereum can go to Aztec (and back), lets people turn any token straight into Aztec gas, and replaces the two old tabs with the three-step wizard you chose. It ships on testnet with documentation for how to deploy it; mainnet comes later as its own step.

What changes, in plain words

One vault per token, stamped out on demand

On Ethereum, a small "factory" contract creates a tiny vault for a token the first time anyone bridges it. Vaults have no settings and no owner — nothing to repoint or hijack. The factory is the only thing that can announce a new token to Aztec.

One Aztec contract for every token

On Aztec, a single "hub" listens for those announcements, creates the matching Aztec token itself, and handles every claim and withdrawal. Its addresses are all predictable in advance, so a fake vault or a fake token can never be slipped in.

Gas from any token

"Send USDC and arrive with gas" already existed as a small toggle. Now any token can become gas (all of it, or a slice), and the app proposes the slice for you ("enough for ~20 transactions").

The wizard

Token → Amount → Review. Logos for known tokens, a paste-an-address path for the rest, plain-language privacy, and a review that shows five lines before you sign. First-time tokens get one extra step, once, ever.

Why the heaviest planning tier

Scored high on four of six risk dimensions: novelty (a contract deploying other contracts on Aztec has no prior art in this repo), blast radius (every bridged asset flows through the hub), irreversibility (the contracts are immutable — a mistake means a new generation), and security. Migration is medium (no production users; the old bridge simply retires) and external coupling is high (Uniswap, Permit2, the Aztec message system, a public token list). Three independent drafts (Claude, Codex, a second Claude), a contradiction check, two audit rounds and a final fresh reviewer went into this version.

Things worth knowing

Private first claims take two transactions

Only for the very first private depositor of a token nobody has bridged before: registration lands first, the claim follows a block later, and the app walks you through both. Public first claims are one transaction; pre-created tokens never hit this. It comes from the standard Aztec token's design, not from the bridge.

Exits burn before they finish

Withdrawing burns on Aztec first, then finishes on Ethereum days later. If the guardians pause, they pause exits on Aztec too, so no new burn can start while payouts are frozen; an exit already burned simply waits until unpause. The review says so; your history never loses the record. A token that switches on a transfer tax after it was bridged charges that tax to the withdrawer — the vault's reserve is protected either way.

What the final reviewer changed

A fresh Codex pass gave a conditional approve. Its conditions are folded in: registration can no longer leave a half-finished token behind (the announcement is checked before anything is created), every live contract's wiring is read back and pinned before promotion, resuming an old transfer re-checks the token's frozen name against the factory, and the Aztec build is compiled in CI and compared to what's committed.

Names are read once

The factory reads a token's name and symbol the moment its vault is created and freezes them (31 plain characters). A token that renames itself later keeps its original Aztec name.

What was deliberately not built

Per-token Aztec contracts, an owner-updatable settings contract, an expiring pause, a legacy exit path, fee-on-transfer support, a Uniswap v3 route, exotic multi-hop routes, a bundled-only token list, a forked token contract, a registration relayer, and anything inside the wallet — the tools app is a plain dApp and the wallet must serve it through its public interface like any other.
