The CI flakes: what was actually breaking, what's fixed and proven, and the one decision left for you

Plain-language companion to the plan at implementations-plan/e2e-deflake/plan.md. Everything below was done autonomously under your /goal directive, with every design choice audited by two independent reviewers (three review rounds; final verdict: conditional approve, all conditions applied).

What happened

Six different tests were failing CI runs at random; none of them were actually random

I pulled the job logs for every red CI run from the last ten days — including the "first attempt failed, re-run went green" ones that never show up as failures. Fifteen red jobs. Fourteen were tests timing out on a wait; one was a download hiccup in CI setup. Every single timeout traced to a wait that was watching the wrong thing — polling a screen element that only appears after some background work the test never accounted for. None of them was a wrong assertion, and none was fixed by waiting longer.

Two big surprises along the way: the "infra boot retry" theory from the goal brief turned out to be a mirage (a known log-reading trap — the retry never actually fired), and the most active flake reproduced on our own idle machine on the first try, which let me instrument it and catch the real mechanism red-handed instead of guessing from CI logs.

The ledger

Each flake, its real cause, and its status

Profile-reset screen never appearing FIXED + PROVEN

4 CI reds, and the one that reproduced locally. The test navigates to the reset screen and waits 5 seconds for a checkbox — but the app was quietly yanking the user back to the home screen mid-navigation (a navigation race, not slowness: the test's "did we arrive?" check passed before the router finished, and a competing redirect then won). The helper now requires the reset screen to arrive and stay put, re-navigates once if the race hits, and dumps a full route history if anything still goes wrong.

Proof: failed 2 of 2 runs before the fix, passed 3 of 3 consecutive solo runs after — with retries turned off.

Post-reset cleanup waits racing the delete FIXED + PROVEN

Deleting a profile does a full, awaited purge that can legitimately take a while — but three tests were waiting on the screen redirect with a budget sized for a quick hop. They now watch the purge's own completion record in storage (prove the profile existed, then require its row, its deletion marker, and all its data gone), and only then expect the redirect — which is instant at that point.

Proof: same 3-of-3 consecutive green runs; the fourth call site (a smoke test) passes in 7 seconds inside its original budget.

Balance checks that could lie FIXED + PROVEN

Two tests hammered "refresh balances" up to 40 times and scanned the whole page for the text "1,000" — which can false-match "$1,000.00", and worse, an imported backup already contains "1,000", so the check could pass without proving the wallet actually re-synced. The new wait requires a balance record that is both fresh (written after the reopen) and exactly correct, then confirms the screen shows it. Bonus: the review process itself caught a bug in my first version of this fix (a retry that could stall forever) before it ever reached CI.

Proof: 3-of-3 consecutive green runs of both affected tests, retries off.

Approve-transaction popup clicked too early FIXED + PROVEN

Two tests clicked the Confirm button with a generic 10-second wait, but Confirm only becomes clickable after fee setup finishes — which on a cold CI machine takes longer than 10 seconds. The helper now waits on the button's own "ready" state (the exact signal the app maintains), with budgets taken from the identical wait elsewhere in the suite, and records timing evidence on every run.

Proof: 3-of-3 consecutive green runs including the real-proving canary; 12 timing samples confirm the ready-signal fires in under half a second on a warm machine — the budget covers CI's measured cold-start penalty.

CI downloading a tool it never uses REMOVED + GUARDED

Every CI run downloaded a blockchain toolkit (Foundry) from an unpinned "latest" URL — eight times per PR — and one bad gateway response killed a whole job. It turns out nothing ever used that download: the Aztec installer already ships its own pinned copy with retries. The step is deleted, and a new guard asserts the real toolchain is present so any future regression fails loudly at setup with a named cause.

Proof: workflow linter green; the certification runs exercise the full pipeline against the change.

Backup-restore smoke test (the 90-second wait) OPEN — YOUR CALL

6 CI reds, at least a month old. Root cause found and confirmed in the code: after restoring a backup, the app won't show the home screen until it finishes a sync against the public testnet — so when that public server has a bad evening, the test times out no matter how healthy CI is. Every real fix touches app code, which the audits froze for this arc; simply raising the wait is banned by your directive and wouldn't survive a long outage anyway. It got full diagnostics (the next red will explain itself), and the decision is yours — see below.

Certification

How "actually fixed" gets proven

One PR into dev, labeled so CI runs everything (both e2e suites plus quality). Then three empty commits, one at a time, each requiring: all three status checks green, first attempt only, zero hidden test retries, zero infra re-boots (checked in the raw logs), no skipped jobs. Any red resets the count to zero. Two-of-three with the third explained counts as a checkpoint, never as done.
