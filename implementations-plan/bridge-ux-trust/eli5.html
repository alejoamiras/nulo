bridge-ux-trust

The bridge works, but it feels rough: private transfers ask you to sign the same thing over and over, deposits and withdrawals live in two separate clunky forms with no balances, and if you refresh mid-bridge you can't tell what's still in flight — worse, the app might silently fire a wallet prompt at you for an old transfer. This arc fixes all three: fewer signatures (a one-time "your wallet signs predictably" check is remembered per wallet), one swap-style form (From Ethereum ⇄ To Aztec, live balances, one-click flip, a private toggle), and a visible journal of every in-flight bridge as a card with an explicit Claim / Finish / Discard button — nothing auto-fires after a refresh. Along the way it structurally closes the two storage-trust issues the last security audit flagged.

Why this tier was chosen

deep, picked by you and confirmed by the rubric: this rewrites the bridge's persistence layer (migration cost HIGH), touches a bearer credential — a secret that IS the money (security sensitivity HIGH), and redesigns the whole bridge UI (blast radius HIGH). Three HIGH dimensions ⇒ deep: three independent plans (me + codex + a fresh Fable agent), consolidation with a decision ledger, two contradiction-check rounds, two double audits, and a final fresh-context codex pass. The ceremony paid for itself: round 1 caught a contradiction that would have stranded every pre-existing private transfer, and the fresh-eyes round 2 found a genuine fund-redirect attack (the "legacy downgrade") that every earlier round missed.

Phases

Phase 1: Foundations (pure logic, no UI)

Build the journal (the structured list of in-flight bridges), the sealed "envelope" (the claim secret AND who it belongs to, encrypted together so browser storage can't lie about the owner), and the per-wallet trust memory (remembers your wallet passed the sign-twice check so it never asks twice again). All unit-tested, including the attack cases.

Phase 2: Rewire the flows

Deposits and withdrawals read/write journal records instead of one fragile slot. A bridge is "done" only when ITS OWN transaction is confirmed on-chain — never because a balance moved. Nothing rediscovered after a refresh ever fires a wallet prompt by itself. The old UI keeps working through this phase, so every commit ships.

Phase 3: The new face

The two old cards die. One swap-style form: From/To with live balances on both sides, a flip arrow, a PRIVATE BRIDGING toggle, and a separate "GET TEST USDC ON SEPOLIA (L1)" button whose copy explicitly says it is NOT the Faucet tab (that one drips a different token inside Aztec). Below it, the journal: one card per in-flight bridge with its stage and one explicit action button.

Phase 4: Smoke, polish, handoff

A browser-less smoke test pins the big behaviors (migration works, nothing auto-claims, selectors stable), logging is swept for secrets, then the manual testnet checklist comes to you — eight scripted walks, including "first private deposit asks exactly 2 signatures, second asks exactly 1".

Human context

Two facts drive most decisions. First: a private deposit's claim secret is a bearer credential — whoever holds it can take the funds — so it's encrypted ("sealed") to your Ethereum signature before anything irreversible happens, and now its owner and amount are sealed WITH it, so tampering with browser storage can't redirect a claim. Second: your wallet must sign the same message identically every time for that recovery to work — that's what the sign-twice self-test proves, and what the new per-wallet memory makes a one-time event. The journal exists because bridges are slow (minutes of proving): you WILL close the tab mid-bridge, and what you find when you come back should be an honest list of cards, not a surprise wallet popup.

Open questions (your call at approval)

  • A1 — finished cards keep their sealed secret until you click Clear (auto-cleaned after 7 days). Insurance against a faked "done"; the secret is spent and useless after a genuine claim. Alternative: wipe immediately on done.
  • A2 — pre-existing private transfers (from before this arc) get a one-time "claim with unverified details" path behind a loud warning, instead of being discard-only. This knowingly trusts old unauthenticated data ONCE to avoid stranding your in-flight testnet funds.
  • A3 — web-security headers (CSP) stay deferred to the later hardening pass. Honest framing: a malicious script on the page remains the one theft-capable attacker until that lands — fine for a trusted internal testnet tool, not for promotion beyond that.
