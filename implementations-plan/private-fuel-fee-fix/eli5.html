Private-fuel claim fee fix

When you bridge tokens "with fuel" privately, the arriving Fee Juice is supposed to pay for its own claim transaction. On V5 that claim reverts with "Amount too low to cover gas cost." Three independent codex reviews proved this is not a bug in Wonderland's contract (your hunch) and not a V5 gas-model break — the contract's check is correct. The real causes are in our code: the wallet commits a fee ceiling that drifts from what it budgeted, and our "is this enough fuel?" floor is measured against the wrong number. The fix pins the ceiling, recalibrates the floor from real on-chain measurements, and proves it with a live private claim run repeatedly until stable.

Why this tier was chosen

Light (you chose it). Rubric: blast radius MODERATE (the fix touches the wallet's shared fee finalizer, used by all embedded-FPC payments) and security-sensitivity MODERATE (under-fund → tx fails; over-fund → you forfeit unused fuel to the FPC) — borderline mid, but the diagnosis is precise (3 codex agents pinned exact lines) and you chose the strong fee-methods network-e2e gate, so light-with-that-gate is sound.

Phases

Phase 1: A reliable, pinned fee cap for the claim (wallet)

Two things can fail: the fuel contract rejects the claim if the bridged fuel is below the fee ceiling (budget), AND the protocol rejects the tx if the committed max-fee is below the live fee at inclusion (timing). The claim needs a max-fee with headroom (so it survives fee rises during the minutes-long proving window — your instinct), and that value must be pinned (today the wallet re-fetches and overwrites it). We add the headroom for the private fuel claim specifically, and pin it — without changing the cap other apps rely on.

Proves itself by: wallet fee unit tests (explicit-fee commits exactly that; embedded-without-fees reuses the cap with no drift; non-embedded keeps the 1.5× default) + typecheck + lint.

Phase 2: Measure the real private claim + find the right headroom (run it ’til stable)

Our test script only exercised the public claim. Add the private one and, per run, log the gas limit, the committed max-fee, the resulting ceiling, and the actual fee — separately, so we know whether the ceiling is driven by the fee cap or by the two-call bundle's gas. Find the smallest headroom multiplier where the claim settles (lands on-chain) every time, across 3+ runs in different fee conditions, then set the minimum-fuel floor from the resulting ceiling.

Proves itself by: the private claim SETTLING (included) on V5 every run at the chosen multiplier; per-run logs decomposing the ceiling; a stable floor ≥ the worst observed ceiling.

Phase 3: Apply it + manifest + the RIGHT e2e gate

Bake in the calibrated headroom + floor (candidate → promote). Then gate on an e2e that actually sends an embedded fee-payer claim to inclusion and checks the committed cap — the path that regressed. (The fee-methods suite we first picked turned out not to exercise this path.)

Proves itself by: faucet typecheck + unit + lint green; the embedded fee-payer settlement e2e green; a fresh private fueled deposit self-pays end-to-end.

Human context

Two fee numbers matter: the actual fee a tx costs, and the ceiling (gas limit × max fee-per-gas) — the most it could cost. Wonderland's private FPC must be solvent for the ceiling before it pays, then refunds the difference — so the bridged fuel must cover the ceiling, not just the actual fee (our old floor used the actual fee — too low). Separately, the committed max-fee must stay ≥ the network's live fee at inclusion, or the protocol rejects the tx — and the claim lands minutes after it's built (proving), so the fee can move. Your instinct was right and codex's first review confirmed it: the max-fee needs headroom, not a rock-bottom value — otherwise it's underpriced by the time it lands. We give the private claim that headroom (smallest reliable amount, measured on real V5), pin it so it can't drift, and size the bridged fuel to cover the resulting ceiling. Headroom is scoped to this claim so other apps that rely on the tight cap aren't disturbed.
