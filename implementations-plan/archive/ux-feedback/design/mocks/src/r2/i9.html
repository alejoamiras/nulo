Round 2No toggletips, no inline lines. What's left is the plain pattern: a dotted underline on the term, a one-sentence tooltip on hover or keyboard focus, and a glossary page for everything. Hover any dotted word below; the pinned ones show the open state. The full list of where tooltips go is in the tooltip map.

How a dotted term behaves

  • At rest: a dotted grey underline, no icon. Hover: the underline brightens and the tooltip opens after a short delay. Keyboard focus opens it at once; Esc closes it; the pointer can move onto it without it vanishing (WCAG 1.4.13).
  • Nothing inside is clickable. That's the rule that separates it from the toggletips you didn't like, and it's the same line Carbon ("definition tooltip") and Atlassian draw (Carbon, Atlassian).
  • Screen readers get the sentence via aria-describedby. In running text the term is a tab stop; inside a form it isn't, so Tab still goes field to field.

Why the glossary page too

  • A tooltip must never be the only place something is said (NN/g). Every dotted term has a glossary entry with the same sentence, so hover-only content is never the sole source.
  • It also holds words that appear too often to underline everywhere: private and public balance, proving.
  • It lives in Settings → App, since tooltips can't carry a "learn more" link.

My pick: dotted terms + the glossary page

The dotted term is the lightest help that still works on first sight: it changes nothing about the layout and tells the eye "there's more here" without an icon. The glossary is what makes it safe, because nothing lives only in a tooltip. Five terms get the underline to start (the map lists them); a sixth needs a glossary entry first and your sign-off.

Confidence: high on the pattern and the no-links rule; moderate on the glossary's place in Settings.
