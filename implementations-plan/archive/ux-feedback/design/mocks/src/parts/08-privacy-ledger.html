The privacy strip looks like a row of checkboxes

Send pageReview sheet
Your noteOn the send pop-up the rectangles for representing "hidden" feel like checkboxes and it's extremely confusing.

What the code does

  • Above the send button, PublishStrip.vue shows You / To / Amount, each with a 7px square: hollow means hidden, filled means public, orange means your address leaks through the fee (publish-mark.module.css).
  • An empty square next to a word is the universal checkbox. Here the empty ones are the good news, and nothing on the strip says what it's about.
  • Clicking it opens the review sheet with a sentence per row; a send whose fee exposes you goes through that sheet first ("Review send").

What the research says

  • No product surveyed uses hollow and filled squares for visibility. Audience selectors use a padlock for "only me" and a globe for "everyone" (Venmo, Facebook, Google Drive), which is already Nulo's own pair.
  • Abstract labels fail: in a study of Apple's privacy labels, 1 of 12 people understood "Data Used to Track You" and 11 of 12 understood "Data Not Linked to You" (arXiv).
  • Zcash's wallet checklist: warn at the moment an action newly exposes something (checklist).

What changes

  • The strip gets a plain heading, "What everyone will see", and each cell shows the value an observer would get: the real address or amount when it's public, •••••• when it isn't. Your address in orange when the fee gives it away.
  • No squares, no icons to decode. The masked dots borrow the password-field convention for "hidden".
  • A fee contract Nulo can't vouch for shows "Can't tell" instead of dots.

Clicks

Unchanged: the strip opens the review sheet, and an exposing send still goes through it.

Effort: small to medium. One component and its vocabulary; the review sheet's rows switch to the same cells.

My pick: A

A shows the answer instead of encoding it: nobody has to learn what a mark means when the cell literally says •••••• or 0x1dd7…d930. The heading tells a first-time user what the strip is for, which none of the other options do as directly. B is the right fallback if you want a one-line PR. C is calm but gives up the breakdown that makes the exposed-fee case understandable.
