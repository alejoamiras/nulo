No-fuel claim fee source — spend the gas you earned

You proved the private fueled bridge works: a fresh wallet bridges + fuels privately, and the claim pays its own gas. That leaves you with private Fee Juice — gas held privately at the PrivateFPC. This plan lets you spend it: when you do a later bridge without "arrive with gas", the claim should pay its fee from whatever gas you have — private first, public as fallback — instead of saying "no gas" because it only ever looked at your public balance.

Review outcome (codex)

The first draft was rejected — and rightly so. Codex caught that my original design tried to make the faucet reimplement fee selection it can't actually control (the wallet auto-defaults to a sponsor, and the "are you sure you have enough gas?" check wasn't tied to what actually gets charged). That pushed a rewrite to the simpler, correct shape (private = faucet pays automatically; public = hand to the wallet's own fee screen). Round 2: conditional approve, with two precision conditions now folded in (estimate the fee with the fee-payment attached so it's not under-counted; keep the wording honest that "public" means "unblocked, you choose in the wallet"). Full trail in plan.md → Audit verdicts.

What's actually broken (plain version)

Think of two wallets for gas: a public jar and a private jar. The private fuel claim fills your private jar. But the no-fuel bridge claim only checks the public jar — sees it empty — and refuses, even though your private jar has plenty. The fix teaches the claim to look in both jars and spend from the private one first.

One real trade-off you're choosing (private-first): paying from the private jar via the FPC does not give change back — you pay the full reserved gas amount each time (≈ 1.5× the actual fee, the safety margin), even if the real fee was smaller. You picked private-first for privacy; this is the cost. The alternative is "always let the wallet ask me per claim" (you pick public/private/sponsored each time). Confirm at approval.
A subtlety the review (codex) caught: the wallet has no way for an app to say "pay from my public gas jar" — only "use this private fee contract." So the fix splits cleanly: when you have private gas, the faucet pays from it automatically; when you only have public gas, the faucet simply stops blocking and lets the wallet's own fee screen handle it (you pick "Public Fee Juice" there — the wallet already supports this). Either way you're unblocked.

Phases

Phase 1: bridge-core gets the "pay from private gas" tool

Add the thin wrapper (FPCFeePaymentMethod) + the private-balance reader bits in bridge-core, right next to the existing fee-payment wrappers it already owns. Nothing user-facing yet — just the building block.

Proves itself by: bridge-core typecheck + a small unit test (the wrapper points at the right FPC) + lint, all green.

Phase 2: the no-fuel claim looks in both jars

A small, pure decision function — "given public balance, private balance, and the fee, which jar do I spend?" (private first; public → hand to the wallet's picker; "couldn't read a balance" → say "try again", never a false "no gas"; truly empty → a clear shortfall message). For the private case the faucet supplies the fee with the exact gas amount it checked, so the pre-flight check actually matches what gets charged. Plus: widen the wallet permission list (two narrow, named functions) so the claim is allowed to read the private balance and pay from it.

Proves itself by: faucet typecheck + 7+ unit tests on the decision function (private-only, public-only, both→private, neither, exact-boundary, unreadable→retry) + a manifest-scope test + lint, all green.

Phase 3: prove it on the real testnet

Extend the existing testnet script: earn private gas via a fuel claim, then do a no-fuel claim that pays from that earned private gas — and confirm it settles on V5 and the private balance went down. Same way we proved the private claim itself.

Proves itself by: the script prints settlement (✅) for a no-fuel claim paid from private gas + the FPC balance dropped, on live testnet. (The faucet→wallet wiring is proven by your manual UI test, like last time.)

Human context

The "PrivateFPC" is a small fee-payment contract (from Wonderland) that the wallet already knows about and already uses to pay your private fuel claim. It can hold a private gas balance for you and pay future fees from it via a function called pay_fee. The only reason the no-fuel claim couldn't use it yet is that (1) the faucet never read that balance, and (2) the wallet's permission list for this app didn't include the two functions needed (balance_of to read it, pay_fee to spend it). Both are narrow, named permissions on one pinned contract — no broad access.
