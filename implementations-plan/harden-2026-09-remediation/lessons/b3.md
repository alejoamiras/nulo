## 2026-09-14 — recon + competing outline
Mid tier: one Sonnet sweep (`b3-isolation/recon.md`), one Sonnet mapper on the DEK/session lifecycle, and the same-family competing outline (`outline-fable.md`, Fable 5.1). The mapper found the design question the seed's "accepted" hides: mixing the DEK into the PXE and MAC keys widens "degraded" (DEK-less, `dek === undefined`) from imported-accounts-only to wallet-wide PXE and dApp sessions, with no UI surface today (one toast) and two silent hazards — a placeholder key would quarantine-delete every stored session (the derivation must THROW so `verifyOrDrop` hides instead), and the fresh-DEK mint at password change would rotate the PXE key under the user. The outline resolved both (throwing provider, refuse the mint, `recoveryMode` banner) and settled the journal fork (gate the two reads at the RPC boundary; writes leave `rpcMethods`; internal writers legitimately target non-active profiles) and F-14 (sender checks with an exact-offscreen-URL narrowing; a Port buys nothing against a same-extension page). ## 2026-09-14 — blueprint dual audit (codex round 1 + fable round 1)
Both `reject`, on the same blocking item neither the seed nor the outline had seen: once the DEK is load-bearing, a corrupted `dekSealed` has NO repair path — `exportBackupMaterial` refuses and points at password change (`profile/service.ts:1802`), the plan made password change point at export, and passkey restore throws at `:2420` with no phrase to fall back on. Adopted fable's fix (export a fresh DEK when the slot will not unseal; passkey restore tolerates an unopenable blob) plus codex's truthful messages. Codex's other blockers, all verified and adopted: the warm PXE bypass (store keys/runtimes survive lock and profile switch → `setProfileRecoveryMode` gates new admission), reflected offscreen responses (the service accepts any same-extension request and replies to the supplied `from` → request-direction check), journal wire events broadcast every profile's records (→ filtered emits), stale sync deleting whatever row holds the id after the node await (→ re-read and compare the tuple). Disagreements settled in `ledger.md`: journal events gated (codex) rather than documented (fable); tx-hash reconciliation scoped (codex) rather than left as the "safe over-claim" (fable); helper shape narrowed to fable's base-key block. Lesson: the same-family leg found the dead end from the USER's side (what can a stranded profile still do?) while codex found the runtime bypasses — the two legs are not redundant.

## 2026-09-14 — blueprint codex round 2 (final pass on the ledger)
Verdict `reject`, three blocking + one should-fix, all follow-throughs on recovery mode and all adopted: (1) passkey EXPORT also refuses an undecryptable DEK (`profile/service.ts:1708-1715`) — it now seals a fresh DEK under the ceremony wrap key instead; (2) full-backup assembly dials PXE through `AccountStateService.backup()` for every Active network, so recovery mode would abort the very export the repair path needs — the service omits that network's state the way it already omits a non-Active one, and the export flow reports it; (3) the toggle RPC had no admission-order guarantee (events publish to clients before un-awaited local subscribers) and its second-chain test passed with the check deleted — replaced by an SW-side admission guard in `PxeServiceClient.request`, backed by a synchronous `profileService.isRecoveryMode`, tested with an immediate request against the already-open chain; (4) stale text (tab-bound rejection vs Firefox's tab-hosted document; prefix match; "harmless" hidden-key overwrite) corrected. Lesson: a recovery path has to be walked end to end from the stranded user's first click to a healthy session — each leg (export, assembly, restore) was individually "fixed" and still did not connect.
