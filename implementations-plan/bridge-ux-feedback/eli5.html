bridge-ux-feedback

Your testnet session surfaced four rough edges in the new bridge: the private Aztec balance hides until you flip the toggle, a claimed in-flight card gives no sign of life, finished cards linger until you discard them by hand, and "→ AZTEC" doesn't read as a direction. This round fixes all four — the engine now narrates each step of a claim onto its card (unsealing → syncing → sending → confirming → verifying), finished cards quietly leave on their own, every card states both ends of the journey, both Aztec balances are always visible, and every transaction gets an explorer link. Plus the worst offender dies: the alarming "couldn't be confirmed" dead-end after three silent minutes becomes a live, truthful "still confirming" that keeps checking.

Why this tier was chosen

That paid off: my draft's "finished means the secret is worthless, safe to delete" claim was proven WRONG by both auditors, and the fix (hide, never delete) is strictly better.

Phases

Phase 1: The engine learns to narrate

The journal engine already knows exactly what it's doing at every moment — it just never told anyone. It gains a "step" signal (unsealing → syncing → sending → confirming → verifying) the card can display live, the 3-minute false alarm becomes a patient ~30-minute watch in short rounds (so the buttons stay usable), a dead RPC node reads as "node unreachable" instead of a stuck claim, and finished bridges hide themselves after a short ✓ moment — without deleting anything (the 7-day cleanup keeps the safety net).

Phase 2: The cards tell the story

Card headers become real journeys ("ETHEREUM → AZTEC"), a spinner + live status line shows the narration, every transaction hash links to its explorer page, finishing shows a ✓ plus a toast, and every warning state carries an honest one-liner about what is and isn't at risk — phrased per state, never blanket comfort.

Phase 3: Both balances, always

The Aztec panel shows Public and Private balances stacked, with the active one highlighted by the toggle (the arbitrated winner — all three reviewers independently picked it). You'll never choose a direction blind again.

Human context

The "stuck" card you saw was actually two things: a claim that completed but stayed listed by design (a safety rule from the parent plan: keep finished records in case "finished" was faked), and a progress window that was genuinely invisible. The fix keeps the safety rule's teeth (records are hidden, not destroyed — deletion still requires your click or the 7-day cleanup) while giving you the finished-and-gone experience you asked for. The yellow note you hit was a timeout message firing three minutes into a process that simply takes longer on testnet — it now keeps watching and says so.
