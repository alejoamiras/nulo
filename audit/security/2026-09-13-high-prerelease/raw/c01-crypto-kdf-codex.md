## Non-findings

- **NF-1 — PBKDF2 runs per encryption; IV-derived salt provides 96 bits of uniqueness. Confidence: high.** Every `EncryptionKey.encrypt()` draws a fresh 12-byte IV, hashes it, and runs PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 with 600,000 iterations; decryption repeats that derivation (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/encryption-key.ts:15`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/encryption-key.ts:41`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/encryption-key.ts:75`). Sealing guard, master, and entropy therefore costs three separate derivations, plus another for the password-wrapped DEK (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/password-secret-box.ts:201`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/service.ts:1975`). Hashing the IV does **not** turn its 96 random bits into 256 random salt bits. Repeated IVs under the same password would repeat both key and nonce; the birthday approximation is `q(q−1)/2^97`. No mechanism forcing repetitions was found. Different IVs prevent straightforward reuse of a computed PBKDF2 result across ciphertexts. An attacker can test a password against one slot, so multiple slots do not multiply the minimum guessing cost. The configured iteration count matches OWASP’s PBKDF2-SHA256 recommendation; the documented preference for stronger password choices remains material. [OWASP password-storage guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Password_Storage_Cheat_Sheet.html#pbkdf2)

- **NF-2 — AAD covers profile slots and account exports; full backups omit it. Confidence: high.** Guard, master, entropy, and DEK use distinct purpose tags (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/password-secret-box.ts:56`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/imported-keys-dek-box.ts:29`). Account exports authenticate the complete serialized payload under `nulo:account-export:v1` (`packages/aztec-runtime/src/account/account-export.ts:35`, `packages/aztec-runtime/src/account/account-export.ts:146`). Full-backup encryption/decryption omit AAD (`apps/extension/src/popup/pages/settings/security/export/full.vue:338`, `apps/extension/src/composables/useFullBackupImport.ts:396`), but AES-GCM still authenticates their ciphertext, and backup interpretation applies checksum/compatibility gates (`apps/extension/src/composables/useFullBackupImport.ts:84`). No cross-purpose acceptance path was demonstrated: the other inspected consumers require their distinct AAD. Purpose-only profile AAD does not prevent same-slot or whole-profile substitution; the envelope MAC supplies identity binding, subject to NF-16.

- **NF-3 — Passhash remains password-equivalent, but current production paths do not persist or transmit it as an RPC credential. Confidence: high for inspected paths.** `getPasshash()` is exactly unsalted SHA-256 of UTF-8 password bytes (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/encryption-key.ts:122`). Possession permits the same PBKDF2 derivations without knowing the password and permits fast checking of password guesses against the passhash itself. Current profile operations derive it inside the service worker; full-backup operations derive it locally in the popup (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/service.ts:613`, `apps/extension/src/popup/pages/settings/security/export/full.vue:334`, `apps/extension/src/composables/useFullBackupImport.ts:390`). `SessionManager.open()` uses only its presence as a signal and persists a random bearer instead; legacy passhash sessions are rejected (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/session-manager.ts:253`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/session-manager.ts:553`). Passwords and passkey PRF output do cross the internal extension RPC boundary, which checks sender identity/origin (`packages/extension-messaging/src/background/service.ts:38`, `packages/extension-messaging/src/core/sender-auth.ts:17`). Request logging omits parameter values, and logger redaction includes password/passhash/PRF (`packages/extension-messaging/src/core/base-service.ts:106`, `packages/extension-messaging/src/core/envelope-summary.ts:55`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/logger/utils.ts:108`). No concrete passhash logging or storage leak was found. README claims describing it as public or persisted are stale (`packages/wallet-crypto/README.md:18`, `packages/wallet-crypto/README.md:55`).

- **NF-4 — Variable-time comparisons exist, without a demonstrated secret-recovery oracle. Confidence: moderate.** `array_equals()` exits at the first mismatch (`packages/wallet-core/src/utils/arrays.ts:1`). Its guard comparison follows successful AES-GCM authentication and compares a public constant (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/password-secret-box.ts:218`). The entropy/master pairing comparison involves secrets, but both inputs have already been decrypted, and no reachable way to submit progressively chosen plaintext prefixes without the wrapping credential was identified (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/service.ts:1926`). Envelope MAC verification delegates to WebCrypto rather than comparing tag bytes in JavaScript (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/entropy-mac.ts:108`). Account-export checksum and fingerprint comparisons concern public comparands, not authentication secrets (`packages/aztec-runtime/src/account/account-export.ts:141`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/service.ts:2007`).

- **NF-5 — Fixed zero HKDF salts do not expose the high-entropy roots; purpose domains are distinct. Confidence: high.** Envelope authentication uses a length-checked `master‖dek` input and `nulo:envelope-mac:v3`; imported account encryption uses the DEK and `nulo:imported-account-key:v2|…` (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/entropy-mac.ts:39`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/entropy-mac.ts:61`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/imported-account-key-box.ts:24`). Other HKDF expand labels are distinct: `nulo:session-wrap:v1`, `nulo:master:v1`, `nulo:dek-wrap:v1`, and `nulo:pxe-store:v1` (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/session-secret-box.ts:50`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/passkey-credential.ts:30`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/pxe-store-key.ts:20`). No AES-GCM/HMAC key reuse was found. HKDF explicitly supports an omitted salt represented by hash-length zero bytes, while `info` binds application context; this does not provide password stretching, but these roots are not raw passwords. [RFC 5869 §§2.2, 3.1–3.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5869)

- **NF-6 — Imported-key `|` delimiter injection does not collide for the production input types. Confidence: high.** The grammar is `prefix|number|string` (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/imported-account-key-box.ts:26`). A numeric chain ID cannot contribute `|`; the entire remaining suffix is the address. Consequently, placing delimiters inside the address cannot shift the chain/address boundary. Stored and restored imported-key rows validate `chainId` as a number, and creation uses the recomputed account address (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/account/spec.ts:105`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/services/account/service.ts:470`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/services/account/service.ts:778`). Calling the bare JavaScript function with an out-of-contract string chain ID could create ambiguity, but no production path supplying that shape was established. The usual `-0`/`0` numeric alias does not represent two distinct chains.

- **NF-7 — The session record intentionally grants both master and imported-key authority. Confidence: high.** `wrapPair()` returns the token alongside the salt and ciphertext, so a complete record reader can decrypt both secrets offline (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/session-secret-box.ts:69`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/session-secret-box.ts:91`). This wrapping protects neither secret from that reader; its useful distinction is that the bearer is random and does not expose the password. Storage is specifically `chrome.storage.session`, and strict mode defaults to enabled (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/session-manager.ts:167`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/config/config.ts:26`). Chrome documents session storage as memory-backed and inaccessible to content scripts by default; no production `setAccessLevel()` broadening was found in the searched scope. The documented accepted residual explicitly includes a passive session-store reader acquiring both authorities (`implementations-plan/key-model-v2-hardening/plan.md:454`). A previously copied bearer remains decryptable after lock; deletion cannot revoke an attacker’s offline copy. [Chrome storage documentation](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/reference/api/storage#property-session)

- **NF-8 — Normal lock, profile replacement, and strict-mode cleanup remove the stored bearer; storage failures remain a limitation. Confidence: high for normal paths, moderate for lifecycle completeness.** Close clears live state and deletes the session record; explicit lock performs a read-back and reports failure if the record remains (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/session-manager.ts:356`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/service.ts:853`). Replacement writes a single successor record and wipes the previous live DEK (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/session-manager.ts:294`). Enabling strict mode asynchronously clears both live and persisted bearer fields, preventing subsequent refresh from rewriting them; startup rejects a bearer when strict mode is enabled (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/session-manager.ts:465`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/session-manager.ts:553`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/session-manager.ts:718`). **Cleanup is not unconditional:** failed deletion can leave a record, and strict-mode cleanup failures are logged and swallowed (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/session-manager.ts:389`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/session-manager.ts:496`). No concrete untrusted trigger bypassing the remaining checks was established. An in-memory simulation passed restore, profile replacement, strict-mode scrub followed by refresh, and explicit close.

- **NF-9 — Passkey separation binds credential ID and PRF, not profile ID, userHandle, or an explicit RP identifier. Confidence: high for derivation; moderate for browser-level isolation.** Salt is `SHA-256("nulo:kdf:v1"‖credentialIdBytes)`; master and DEK wrapping use different HKDF labels (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/passkey-credential.ts:49`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/passkey-credential.ts:79`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/passkey-credential.ts:109`). Identical credential ID and PRF reproduce identical keys regardless of `userHandle`; the executable check confirmed this. Different credential IDs produced different keys with the same synthetic PRF. Service-level unlock checks credential ID, while import rejects duplicate credentials even when userHandle is absent (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/service.ts:770`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/service.ts:2023`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/service.ts:2123`). Ceremonies specify `RP_ID = "nulo.sh"` and require user verification (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/passkey/spec.ts:21`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/utils/passkey-ceremony.ts:48`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/utils/passkey-ceremony.ts:71`). RP isolation is supplied by WebAuthn, not by these HKDF labels. If another context already possesses the same raw PRF and credential ID, it can derive the same keys; no second RP-binding barrier exists. No path allowing a different RP to obtain those inputs was demonstrated. PRF output does reach popup/SW memory (`apps/extension/src/wallet/utils/passkey-ceremony.ts:132`); it should not be described as remaining inside the authenticator. [WebAuthn Level 3 PRF extension](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/#prf-extension)

- **NF-10 — KDF v2 uses the full 64-byte reduction and distinct numeric tuple positions. Confidence: high.** BIP-39 derives 512 bits with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512/2048 and reduces the complete result modulo Fr (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/mnemonic-master.ts:26`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/mnemonic-master.ts:50`). Passkey derivation likewise requests 512 bits before reduction (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/passkey-credential.ts:79`). This avoids the direct 32-byte reduction’s five-versus-six-preimage skew under the pseudorandom-output model; expansion does not create additional source entropy. The Fr field has approximately 253.5967 bits of range (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/reduction-entropy.test.ts:22`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/reduction-entropy.test.ts:80`). Account derivation validates L1 chain ID as `0…2^32−1` and type/index as nonnegative safe integers, rejecting negative, fractional, NaN, infinite, and unsafe inputs (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/derive-account-seed.ts:19`). Type and index occupy separate field positions, so equal indices across different types do not structurally collide. Separator values `2720999938` and `914717451` match the first four big-endian SHA-256 bytes of their labels and did not collide with the installed upstream enum in the executable check (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/nulo-separators.ts:14`). Same-L1 rollup key reuse is explicitly intentional (`implementations-plan/key-model-v2/plan.md:57`). This establishes the derivation boundary, not every downstream transaction chain-binding path.

- **NF-11 — The plaintext fingerprint is an offline candidate oracle and an equality/linkability signal. Confidence: high.** It hashes a fixed label and the master, without a device/profile salt (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/wallet-fingerprint.ts:29`). Someone knowing the first 23 words of a 24-word phrase has exactly eight checksum-valid last-word completions; each candidate can be checked with BIP-39 derivation, reduction, and fingerprint hashing. “One SHA-256 per guessed phrase” omits the BIP-39 computation unless the candidate master is already available (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/mnemonic-master.ts:26`). The exact-eight statement assumes the missing word is the last one. Fingerprints also correlate matching masters across devices or backups wherever values are available, including profiles with no public account history; their derivation has no device scope. This is the documented candidate-confirmation/equality tradeoff, not a newly demonstrated recovery attack (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/wallet-fingerprint.ts:9`, `implementations-plan/key-model-v2-hardening/plan.md:454`). “Never reduces entropy” should be read as a computational-security claim, not a literal information-theoretic claim. No practical attack on an otherwise unknown generated phrase was found.

- **NF-12 — Package zeroization is incomplete, but the remaining copies lack a demonstrated disclosure path. Confidence: high.** Specific unwiped allocations are the password UTF-8 bytes in `getPasshash()` (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/encryption-key.ts:124`), the potentially secret-bearing input bytes in `getHashHex()` (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/encryption-key.ts:134`), the temporary `Buffer.from(token)` used for bearer serialization (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/session-secret-box.ts:93`), and the temporary decoded token Buffer copied into a Uint8Array (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/session-secret-box.ts:120`). Exceptional allocation gaps also precede protected blocks: mnemonic seed copying, passkey OKM copying, and session salt/IV generation after token generation (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/mnemonic-master.ts:51`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/passkey-credential.ts:87`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/session-secret-box.ts:76`). An allocation failure while making the second returned session-secret copy could strand the first copy (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/session-secret-box.ts:139`). Named buffers protected by existing `finally` blocks are wiped; those blocks cannot wipe independent copies. Recommended hygiene is to name and wipe temporary buffers and move post-secret allocations inside protected blocks. Without an attacker-accessible memory disclosure, these remain defense-in-depth observations under the required negative list.

- **NF-13 — Supporting codecs/exports and field internals also retain secret representations. Confidence: high.** `getMnemonic()` leaves expanded entropy arrays to GC (`packages/wallet-core/src/utils/mnemonic.ts:2075`); account export encryption/decryption leaves plaintext byte arrays unwiped (`packages/aztec-runtime/src/account/account-export.ts:148`, `packages/aztec-runtime/src/account/account-export.ts:156`). Full-backup paths retain passhash/plaintext scratch, including stale-operation exits (`apps/extension/src/popup/pages/settings/security/export/full.vue:334`, `apps/extension/src/composables/useFullBackupImport.ts:390`). Base64 conversion necessarily introduces immutable strings in the current implementation (`packages/wallet-core/src/utils/encoding.ts:21`, `packages/wallet-core/src/utils/encoding.ts:34`). Installed Fr/Fq stores immutable bigint values and allocates fresh buffers on serialization; `GrumpkinScalar` aliases Fq (`packages/wallet-crypto/node_modules/@aztec/foundation/src/curves/bn254/field.ts:44`, `packages/wallet-crypto/node_modules/@aztec/foundation/src/curves/bn254/field.ts:73`, `packages/wallet-crypto/node_modules/@aztec/foundation/src/curves/grumpkin/index.ts:10`). Upstream SHA-512 derivation also allocates serialized seed and digest buffers (`packages/wallet-crypto/node_modules/@aztec/foundation/src/crypto/sha512/index.ts:13`). Zeroing caller buffers leaves these representations untouched, consistent with the helper’s stated limitations (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/zeroize.ts:13`). No heap-disclosure exploit was demonstrated.

- **NF-14 — No deterministic nonce reuse or ignored ciphertext version was found. Confidence: high.** Password encryption, imported-account encryption, DEK wrapping, and session wrapping each draw a fresh 12-byte IV (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/encryption-key.ts:42`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/imported-account-key-box.ts:48`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/imported-keys-dek-box.ts:41`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/session-secret-box.ts:78`). Their readers require frame versions `0`, `1`, `1`, and session version `2`, respectively (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/encryption-key.ts:71`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/imported-account-key-box.ts:67`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/imported-keys-dek-box.ts:57`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/session-secret-box.ts:113`). Account-export JSON independently validates its version (`packages/aztec-runtime/src/account/account-export.ts:103`). Short frames that pass the prefix-length check still fail AES-GCM authentication. The nonce test detects deterministic regressions; it does not prove random collisions are impossible (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/nonce-uniqueness.test.ts:17`).

- **NF-15 — Secret randomness uses CSPRNGs; Math.random appears in nonsecret identifiers and retry timing. Confidence: high.** All seven direct wallet-crypto `getRandomValues()` calls generate the four IVs, session token/salt, and imported-key DEK (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/encryption-key.ts:42`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/imported-account-key-box.ts:48`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/imported-keys-dek-box.ts:35`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/imported-keys-dek-box.ts:41`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/session-secret-box.ts:76`). Recovery entropy, WebAuthn challenges, and PXE generation identifiers also use WebCrypto (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/service.ts:532`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/utils/passkey-ceremony.ts:39`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/utils/passkey-ceremony.ts:69`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/spec.ts:100`). `getRandomHex()` uses WebCrypto and correctly rounds up for odd lengths (`packages/wallet-core/src/utils/random.ts:9`). The broader search found Math.random in price retry jitter, a send-page diagnostic ID, and a fee-estimation coalescing ID (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/price/service.ts:372`, `apps/extension/src/popup/pages/send.vue:271`, `apps/extension/src/composables/useFeeEstimationMap.ts:66`). The last is operationally significant, but no secret generation or concrete authorization bypass through its predictability was established.

- **NF-16 — Prior envelope-swap/fingerprint findings are addressed to the explicitly accepted degradation policy. Confidence: high.** MAC v3 authenticates requested profile ID, all four sealed slots, and fingerprint; its key requires both master and DEK (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/entropy-mac.ts:51`, `packages/wallet-crypto/src/entropy-mac.ts:61`). Password unlock verifies using the requested ID; bearer restore refuses a mismatched MAC (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/service.ts:379`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/session-manager.ts:588`). Password unlock deliberately drops the DEK and emits a degradation event instead of always refusing the session (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/service.ts:390`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/service.ts:643`). This is expressly accepted, and the integration test permits that outcome while checking password-change refusal (`implementations-plan/mac-identity-binding/plan.md:25`, `apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/service.integration.test.ts:550`). Fingerprint authentication detects editing when credentials become available; it cannot authenticate the pre-unlock duplicate lookup, which still compares plaintext stored values (`apps/extension/src/wallet/services/profile/service.ts:2004`). These properties should not be described as unconditional rejection or pre-unlock tamper prevention, but no new bypass beyond the recorded residual was demonstrated.

3. **What I missed or underemphasized**

4. **Overconfidence**

- “Arguably stronger than an independent random salt” is unsupported. SHA-256 of a 96-bit IV retains at most 96 bits of salt uniqueness and couples salt repetition to nonce repetition (`packages/wallet-crypto/src/encryption-key.ts:42`). Independent per-encryption salts can also derive separate keys.
- “Every other secret buffer … wiped within microseconds” is contradicted by the instances above and unsupported timing evidence.
- “Fingerprint blinding … independently closed” overstates post-unlock detection; the unauthenticated duplicate lookup remains.
- “No nonce reuse” and “type/index cannot collide” should mean **no deterministic reuse or structural input alias found**, not mathematical impossibility. Random collisions and hash collisions remain possible.
- My own zero-findings conclusion must likewise remain bounded: ordinary-path tests and static traces do not establish exhaustive browser-race or memory-disclosure safety.

5. **Net position**

**Revised finding list: empty; no F-* entries.** Retain **NF-12/NF-13** for concrete zeroization gaps, **NF-8** with the demonstrated transient strict-mode residency clarification, and **NF-16** for accepted authentication/degradation limits. Confidence is **high** in these dispositions, **moderate** in the broader conclusion that this cluster contains no additional exploitable vulnerability.
