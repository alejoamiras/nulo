**Verdict: CONFIRMED (high).** `formatCaipChain`, `formatCaipAccount`, `parseCaipAccount`, `resolveNetworkByChainId` are functionally line-for-line duplicates (only TSDoc wording and the CaipChain/CaipAccount type source differ; extension adds `parseCaipChain`). The deliberate-mirror header documents intent but pins nothing; duplication cost is real.

## Q22: Serialization policy is split across three wallet-core helpers
**Pre-read:** Three "stringify hostile values" implementations exist with divergent bigint (`"123"` vs `"123n"`) and Error shapes — but each is documented as serving a different consumer.
**Verdict: ADJUSTED (moderate).** Instances and divergences are accurate: serialization.ts:24-57 (bigint→`"123"`, Error→`{name,message,stack?,code?,details?}`), jobs/error.ts:60-77 (bigint→`"123n"`, Error→`{__error:true,...}`, truncation), arrays.ts:23-39 (lossy, sorted-key). Adjustments: (1) serialization.ts:1-4 is a documented copy of `@aztec/foundation/json-rpc` — an upstream-mirror constraint; a "shared serialization core" must not alter its wire shape. (2) jobs/error.ts:1-24 documents its divergences as deliberate audit-hardening (never-throws envelope, quota cap), so the drift is partly intentional, not purely silent. (3) arrays.ts `safeStringify` builds in-memory comparison keys, not wire/persistence output — weakest member of the family. Real consolidation surface is narrower than claimed (mainly Error shaping between paths 1 and 2).

Properly framed as a maintainability (temporal coupling) finding, not a correctness report — no DO-NOT-FLAG conflict.
